DISCLOSURE. An earlier smoke test (a scratch script, not kept) ran single / team-lean / team-full over
the 5 labelled claims only, and I saw its headline: team calls = 2x single's; team-lean input tokens
slightly BELOW single's, output tokens ~1.7x, cost ~+15%; team-full input ~+28% over team-lean. Those
are therefore NOT predictions. The claims below are about what that smoke run did not show: the 28
extra claims, the fixed-workflow rung, wall-clock, path quality, the judge, and the live-model case.

PREDICTIONS (each falsifiable by race_tables.md / race_raw.json):

P1  Token ratio holds at n=33. Over all 33 claims, team-lean TOTAL tokens per claim are within +/-10%
    of the single agent's, and team-lean COST per claim is between 1.05x and 1.30x the single
    agent's (output tokens are priced 5x input, and the team emits ~1.7x the output).

P2  The cost ladder is monotone. fixed < single < team-lean < team-full on cost per claim, and the
    fixed workflow costs at most 40% of the single agent per claim.

P3  Full-context hand-offs cost real money. team-full total tokens are between 1.15x and 1.50x the
    single agent's.

P4  Wall-clock goes the OTHER way from the smoke run. The smoke run seemed to show the team faster;
    I believe that was a cold-start artifact (single ran first). With warm-up and interleaving, the
    single agent's median wall-clock per claim is LOWER than team-lean's, by 1.2x-2.5x, because the
    stub model is free and what remains is orchestration (more tools/list round trips, a thread pool,
    more prompts built).

P5  Critical path: single = 3.0 sequential model calls per claim, team = 4.0 (parallel specialists),
    i.e. the team is never faster on the structural latency measure.

P6  Quality parity (plumbing check, not evidence about real models). The team gives the SAME coverage
    decision as the single agent on 33/33 claims; deterministic-assertion pass rates are equal; the
    judge-v2 pass counts are equal; path accuracy on the 5 labelled claims is equal.

P7  Nothing in the team's structure helps on this corpus. I expect the verdict to be "keep the single
    agent", and I expect that verdict to survive when specialists are priced on a cheaper model: team
    with Haiku-class specialists will still cost >= 0.5x of the single agent's cost on the SAME
    expensive manager model, because the manager's two calls re-send the notes plus every report.

P8  (Live model -- UNTESTABLE here, no API key.) With a real model, the team will not beat the single
    agent on decision accuracy by more than one claim out of the 5 labelled, and will cost more per
    claim than the single agent. Re-run with --llm-provider anthropic --yes --repeats 3 to test it.

HOW I'D BE WRONG (what would change the verdict): the team's cost advantage appearing only if a much
cheaper model can do the specialists' jobs at equal quality -- a quality question this offline run
cannot answer -- or if claims were long enough that the single agent's growing scratchpad dominated
its cost (here the notes are ~60 tokens and the passages ~2.5k, so it does not).
