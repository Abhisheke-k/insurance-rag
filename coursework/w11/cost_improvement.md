These barely move because the simulated model effectively reads only its single best passage -- it is nearly blind to passages 4 and 5 by construction. That is *why recall above is the evidence to rely on*, and why a real model, which reads every passage it is given, could be affected in ways this table cannot show.

## Decision: k = 4

- **k=4 shipped** (`CLAIM_TOP_K`, default 4): input tokens -15.8%, projected cost -12.5% per request; recall 99% vs 99% at k=5. One family gives anything up: `exclusion_regulatory_fine` 19/24 vs 20/24 (one case in 520).
- **k=3 rejected** despite -32.4% tokens: `exclusion_regulatory_fine` falls to 11/24 -- the governing passage is missing for 54% of those claims. A saving bought by dropping the evidence is not a saving.
- **Not touched:** `/ask` keeps `TOP_K=5` (this experiment measured claim summaries only).
- **Not measured:** whether a real model's accuracy changes with 4 vs 5 passages. Recall says the right passage is there; it does not say the model uses a fifth passage for something recall cannot see (a carve-out two passages away). The first live check is the Week 6 suite at `CLAIM_TOP_K=4` vs `5`.
