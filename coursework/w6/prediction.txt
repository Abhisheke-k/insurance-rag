Week 6 prediction — written before iterating the judge prompt (v1 -> v2).

v1 agreement with blind human labels: 8/25 = 32% (coursework/w6/judge_v1_results.json).
17 disagreements, 16 of them the judge saying PASS when the human said FAIL.

Reading the disagreements: v1 checks the whole cited chunk for a fixed list of
negation phrases ("does not cover", "excludes", ...). Two distinct blind spots
show up repeatedly:

1. A cited passage that quotes an enumerated exclusion item verbatim (e.g.
   "(f) any extortion demand, ransom payment...") carries no literal negation
   phrase in that specific fragment -- the governing "this policy excludes..."
   sentence lives in an earlier chunk (the clause is long enough to have been
   split by the chunker). v1 sees no cue word and passes it.
2. A cited passage that IS negatively worded but is a CARVE-OUT ("Exclusion
   Code E-17 does not apply where...") gets treated the same as a primary
   exclusion ("does not cover...") by v1's single undifferentiated cue list --
   both contain "not", but a carve-out restores cover rather than removing it.

Prediction: rewriting the judge to (a) read the model's own stated rationale
sentence specifically, rather than the whole cited chunk, (b) recognize a
quoted lettered exclusion item ("(a)"-"(h)") as an implicit exclusion even
with no cue word in view, and (c) distinguish carve-out language from primary
exclusion language, will raise agreement from 32% (8/25) to 70%+ on the same
25 cases.

What I expect it will NOT fix: cases where the cited passage is topically
unrelated to the notes but still contains legitimate exclusion phrasing
somewhere in the wider chunk (e.g. a flood sub-limit question answered from
the cyber exclusion's chunk, which genuinely does say "excludes"). That needs
the separate topical-relevance check also added in v2 -- I expect it to help
those specific cases but not to be as reliable as the polarity fix, since its
topic-marker vocabulary is a short, hand-picked list rather than genuine
topic understanding.
