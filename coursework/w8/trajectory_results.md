## Evidence: `cyber_fire_writeback` is right for the wrong reason

The claim: a ransomware attack triggers a fire in the server room; no ransom
paid, no data lost, cover sought for the physical fire damage only. Expected
decision: **covered**, on the strength of the Section 3 write-back ("this
exclusion shall not apply to physical loss of or physical damage to insured
property arising from fire or explosion which itself results from a Cyber
Incident"). The agent said **covered** -- correct -- and `path_correct` still
fails. Reading `trajectory_raw.json` and the retrieved chunks directly:

- Five chunks came back from the one search the agent ran. The top-scored one,
  `4fa131d2878b9fbf::0004`, is **not** the write-back clause. It is items
  **(d)-(h)** of the cyber exclusion's itemised list -- malicious code,
  regulatory investigations, **(f) the ransom/extortion item**, breach of
  contract, and deliberate employee acts -- with the heading
  `SECTION 3 - WRITE-BACK FOR PHYSICAL DAMAGE` tacked onto the very end,
  a bare heading with no body text (the chunker filled the remaining budget
  with the next heading it found; the write-back's actual sentence landed in
  the *next* chunk, `::0005`, which was not the top hit and was never cited).
- The exclusion clause is one long legal sentence split by the chunker's
  sub-clause fallback (`(a)`-`(h)`) because the whole clause is, by design,
  "far larger than any sensible chunk target" (`scripts/make_sample_pdf.py`).
  That fallback keeps each item intact but does **not** keep the negation cue
  ("this policy excludes...") together with every item it governs -- the cue
  sentence lives in an earlier chunk (`::0002`/`::0003`), items (a)-(c) in
  another, and (d)-(h) in this one. `_NEGATION_CUES` in `app/llm.py` is
  checked against `top.body` alone, so a chunk that happens to hold only the
  back half of the itemised list, with no cue phrase inside it, reads as
  **not excluded** -- not because the write-back was found, but because the
  cue word that would have triggered a denial got chunked into a different
  passage.
- The agent's stated rationale makes the wrong reasoning visible without
  needing to inspect the trace: *"Notes match an insured peril: (f) any
  extortion demand, ransom payment, cryptocurrency transfer or negotiation
  fee made or incurred in response to a threat to a Computer System or to
  Data..."* -- item (f) is an **exclusion**, quoted as if it were a covered
  peril. The label is right; the sentence backing it is not just imprecise,
  it is describing the opposite of what it claims.
- This is not a stable win. The stub's ranking put chunk `::0004` a hair
  above `::0005` (0.1447 vs 0.1388, by lexical overlap with the notes) --
  swap which half of the itemised list a slightly different question's
  wording favours, and the same mechanism denies a claim the write-back
  should cover, for the same non-reason. `tests/test_agent.py`-style
  determinism means this exact case will keep passing every time it is
  re-run, which is precisely why a trajectory check that only looks at the
  label would never catch it.

**On `sequential_flood_e17`:** Week 7's committed write-up (`coursework/w7/results.md`)
names this scenario, not `cyber_fire_writeback`, as the correct-answer-wrong-path
case (citing clause 4.1 instead of 4.3). Re-running Week 7's own script
(`scripts/w7_agent_vs_workflow.py`) against the current checkout reproduces its
decision-accuracy numbers exactly (agent 3/5, fixed 4/5) but **not** that specific
citation: today it grounds correctly in clause 4.3 (`cited_exclusion_id: "4.3"`,
verified by direct inspection, not just the keyword check above). The retrieved
document hash also differs (`4fa131d2878b9fbf` here vs `77e34963221c7200` in the
committed `race_raw.json`), which points to the corpus PDF at commit time
carrying different bytes from a re-generation of `scripts/make_sample_pdf.py`
(unchanged since Week 4) rather than a real behavioural difference -- but the
honest statement is that this one detail does not reproduce today, so it is
not used as this week's evidence; `cyber_fire_writeback`, verified against a
fresh run on this checkout, is.

## Reading the aggregate

- **Tool-choice accuracy (4/5) is doing less than it looks.** The one miss,
  `sequential_flood_e17`, needs a second, targeted search per its own `why`
  field, and the loop (`app/agent.py`) enforces *order* (search before check
  before finalize) but never a search *count* -- so nothing stops a second
  search, and nothing prompts one either. The deterministic stub persona
  (`_agent_turn` in `app/llm.py`) always finalizes after exactly one search
  in every scenario tested; this is a property of the stub, not the loop,
  and re-running with a live model against a scenario built to need
  re-search is the honest next step, not assumed here (same caveat Week 7
  made about its own comparison).
- **Outcome accuracy and trajectory accuracy landed on the same number this
  run (60%) by coincidence, not by construction** -- they disagree on two
  different scenarios in opposite directions (`cyber_fire_writeback`: outcome
  right, path wrong; `malformed_claim_number`: path right, outcome wrong on
  the coverage merits, though right on the thing this scenario actually
  tests -- see the scenario file). A single "accuracy" number would have
  reported neither disagreement.
